Principles

How I approach a project

Patterns that show up across every initiative I've run, regardless of industry.

Align before you build

Get stakeholders agreeing on scope, risk tolerance, and priorities before development starts — not after the first change request. Fewer surprises later, less rework.

Scope for the size of the problem

Compare the cost, timeline, and maintenance burden of a few solution paths before committing to the biggest build available. The right-sized answer usually ships faster.

Trace it to the edges

Check every downstream system and reference table connected to a change, not just the fields named in the original request. That's usually where the real risk hides.

Report status, not noise

Give steering committees a clear, current view of what's validated versus pending, so decisions get made on the actual state of the project, not last week's version of it.

Mar 2024 – Present Rogers Communications In progress

Customer 360 CRM Migration — Rogers Communications

Problem
After a merger, customer records at Rogers were held in separate systems, leaving teams without a single view of the customer. Leadership favoured a big-bang migration for speed, despite the risk of exposing every customer at once.
Solution
Led a phased four-wave data unification effort into a single CRM, using Master Data Management (MDM) practices — extraction, cleansing, matching, deduplication, validation, transformation, and reconciliation — to create one trusted customer record. Used the triple constraint to win unanimous Steering Committee approval for phased waves over a big-bang cutover. Tailored communication by seniority, ran mock migrations through change control to catch issues early, brought real end users into UAT and training, and had Finance validate accuracy in a parallel run before go-live.
Status
In progress — Wave 1 is live and was delivered within budget. Data issues were caught in mock migrations before any real customer was affected, and the go-live retrospective produced two permanent process changes that carry into the remaining waves.
Jan 2023 – Feb 2024 Loblaw Companies

Invoice Processing Automation (RPA) — Loblaw Companies

Problem
Finance and Accounts Payable staff were manually keying vendor invoice data, with pressure to build a full AI solution able to handle any invoice format.
Solution
An RPA solution that ingests invoice PDFs, extracts and validates the data, and posts it to the accounting system. Compared cost, timeline, and maintenance burden against the full-AI option and took the trade-off to the Finance Lead.
Outcome
Won approval for a $300K scoped build over a $900K+ full-AI alternative, live in under five months versus 12+ for the alternative. A three-week pilot extension caught extraction errors on scanned invoices and prevented a production data issue; a post-launch duplicate-entry issue was resolved within a week.
Dec 2021 – Dec 2022 Punjab Insurance

Insurance Policy Management Platform — Punjab Insurance

Problem
Customer Service and Operations staff were absorbing significant time on routine policy changes, and the existing COTS platform could not support real-time coverage changes.
Solution
An internal dashboard used by Customer Service and Operations to manage policy information, nominees, beneficiaries, and eligible benefits — automatic processing for simple changes, employee review for complex ones, instead of a costly custom build.
Outcome
Approved in a single steering committee meeting and delivered within an $800K budget. A premium-calculation defect affecting multi-beneficiary policies was caught in testing and corrected before go-live, and three initially resistant agents ended up demoing the tool to peers during UAT.
Dec 2020 – Nov 2021 Wealthsimple

AI Client Onboarding Automation — Wealthsimple

Problem
Clients applied digitally, but Operations staff reviewed and routed every application by hand. Compliance wanted more human escalation; Product wanted more AI autonomy.
Solution
An AI-assisted review and routing layer on the internal Operations platform, with low-confidence cases routed to Operations for manual review. Joint workshops with Operations, Compliance, Product, Risk, and Technology set agreed risk tiers before development began.
Outcome
Processing time fell from 2–4 days to under 10 minutes. A joint-application routing gap was caught in testing and corrected before it reached a real client, and the platform closed its first two quarters with zero compliance-related audit findings.
